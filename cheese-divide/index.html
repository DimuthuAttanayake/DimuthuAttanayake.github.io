America's cheese divide: Walmart vs Whole Foods

Low prices sometimes come with a different cost.

By Dimuthu Attanayake

On any given day, more than two-thirds of U.S. adults eat cheese, according to the U.S. Department of Agriculture. Most of it arrives melted into pizza, sandwiches, tacos and the mac and cheese that more than half the country eats at least once a week. Per-person consumption hit a record 40.5 pounds in 2023, and the habit shows little sign of slowing.

Where shoppers buy that cheese increasingly tracks income. Walmart's typical customer earns less than $80,000 a year, while nearly half of Whole Foods shoppers earn six figures. The price gap on the shelf is just as stark. An analysis of GroceryDB pricing shows Whole Foods shoppers pay at least $2.50 more per package for most everyday cheeses, and more than $13 extra for specialty varieties such as gouda and blue.

Different cheeses serve different roles. Cheddar and American, the two most popular types, dominate mac and cheese, burgers, and grilled sandwiches, prized for their melt and tang. Mozzarella is a key ingredient in America’s pizza, cream cheese compliments the morning bagel, and parmesan the pasta. Feta and goat cheese appear in salads and grain bowls favoured by younger, specialty-seeking consumers.
