How the countries building AI run on dirty power

While AI promises reshape the future, the development of these models use up large volumes of fossil fuels, challenging the planetary health

Photo by Valeriia Miller/Pexels

By Dimuthu Attanayake

From the financial analysts in New York City to the students in rural schools in the global south, these days share a common excitement for AI models. From OpenAI’s ChatGPT, to Google’s Gemini and Anthropic’s Claude to xAI’s Grok, Large Language Models (LLMs) are crunching numbers and doing eighth grade homework, or even advising on how to deal with a toxic friend. By 2023, ChatGPT was called the fastest growing consumer app in history. Claude is becoming increasingly popular for coding, and AlphaFold2 model developed to predict protein structures by Google’s DeepMind, won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2024.

But, AI models use vast amounts of electricity to train. This analysis matches data from Epoch AI and Our World in Data to estimate the proportion of fossil fuel usage by the countries at the forefront of the AI race. Between 2005 to 2024, the top eight countries consuming energy to train AI, powered at least half of their grid by fossil fuel sources, adding to the global carbon emissions.

United States topped the list of countries in terms of using energy to train AI, with the total coming up an estimated 307 GWh, using up more energy than the next 25 countries combined, on a grid powered by 59 percent fossil fuel. This is equal to the amount of energy that could power roughly 51,000 one bedroom apartments in NYC for an entire year.

Fossil fuel sources, coal, oil and natural gas are considered ‘dirty’ sources of energy, compared to low carbon sources such as renewables and atomic energy, due to their contribution to global carbon emissions.

Training a model to become good at a task, for example to predict protein structures, is a time consuming process that takes weeks, or months. This would happen at AI data centers, sort of massive warehouses with rows after rows of servers holding specialised chips, that would feed on energy to operate.

There is a growing concern on the use of dirty energy to train AI models and the environmental impact of AI data centers, with the International Energy Agency projecting consumption to increase by approximately 15 percent annually.

Last December, over 200 environmental groups, including Greenpeace, Friends of the Earth and Food & Water Watch urged the members of congress to impose a moratorium on building new data centers in the US.

OpenAI founder, Sam Altman, recently defended the energy usage by data centers saying that: “people talk about how much energy it takes to train an AI model – but it also takes a lot of energy to train a human.” But he also admitted the need to generate more energy via cleaner sources such as nuclear, wind or energy.
