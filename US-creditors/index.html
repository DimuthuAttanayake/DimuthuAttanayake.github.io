Who Owns America's Debt?

Who lends America its money, and how has that changed over 25 years? A look at country-level U.S. Treasury holdings from 2000 to 2025.

By Dimuthu Attanayake

Scroll to begin ↓

In 2000, foreign governments and investors held less than $1 trillion in U.S. Treasury securities. That was about to change.

The 2008 financial crisis was the turning point. As the U.S. borrowed to fund stimulus and bank bailouts, foreign holdings surged from $2 trillion to nearly $4 trillion in just three years.

By 2025, the total crossed $9 trillion — a ninefold increase in 25 years.

In the early 2000s, China began aggressively buying U.S. Treasuries, recycling its enormous trade surpluses.

China's holdings peaked at $1.28 trillion in 2013. For a decade, it was America's single largest foreign creditor.

Then Beijing started selling. Trade tensions, a push to diversify reserves, and a slowing economy drove a steady retreat. Today China holds nearly 40% less than its peak.

Japan has been a steady creditor throughout. Even as China surged past it in the mid-2000s, Tokyo kept buying.

By 2019, as China pulled back, Japan reclaimed the top spot by staying the course while its rival retreated.

Today Japan holds $1.16 trillion in U.S. Treasuries. It has been America's largest foreign creditor for seven consecutive years.

Belgium-Luxembourg, the Cayman Islands and Ireland, small economies that double as global financial hubs, collectively hold $1.6 trillion in U.S. Treasuries, surpassing Japan.

These jurisdictions attract capital through low tax rates, light regulation and the financial infrastructure that routes money on behalf of investors worldwide.

The money flowing through these jurisdictions often belongs to sovereign wealth funds, central banks and institutional investors elsewhere, routed through offshore custodial accounts and European clearinghouses like Euroclear in Brussels.

Multilateral holders such as the IMF, World Bank or the European Central Bank, also hold significant amounts of U.S. debt. Which was beyond the scope of this analysis.
